Understanding Periodontal Disease

Periodontal disease is a progressive, chronic inflammatory condition driven by pathogenic subgingival bacterial communities. Beginning as reversible gingivitis characterized by bleeding and erythema, untreated infection advances into chronic periodontitis, destroying the periodontal ligament and adjacent alveolar bone.

Early diagnosis and meticulous ultrasonic root surface debridement arrest the destructive cascade, reducing pathological periodontal pocket depths and preserving the teeth for long-term masticatory function.

Common Signs of Periodontal Breakdown

  • Persistent Bleeding on Brushing or Flossing: Active micro-ulceration within periodontal pockets.
  • Gingival Recession: Exposed tooth root surfaces causing sensitivity and visual elongation of teeth.
  • Chronic Halitosis: Persistent odor caused by volatile sulfur compounds produced by anaerobic subgingival bacteria.
  • Hypermobility & Spacing: Secondary drifting or looseness of teeth caused by progressive loss of bone support.

The Periodontal Treatment Protocol

1. Comprehensive 6-Point Periodontal Probing & Radiography

Full-mouth diagnostic pocket charting and radiographic imaging evaluate horizontal and angular alveolar bone defects.

2. Ultrasonic Scaling & Root Surface Debridement

Precision ultrasonic and piezoelectric instrumentation thoroughly cleanses hard calculus and bacterial endotoxins from root cementum.

3. Periodontal Flap Surgery & Tissue Regeneration (Advanced Cases)

For deep, non-responsive pockets, gentle surgical reflection allows direct visualization, defect debridement, and bone graft placement.

4. Supportive Periodontal Maintenance (SPT)

Tailored recall protocols every 3 to 4 months monitor pocket depths, assess bone stability, and help prevent disease recurrence.
