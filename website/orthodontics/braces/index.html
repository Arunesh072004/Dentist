Clinical Overview & Mechanism

Orthodontic braces utilize biocompatible brackets bonded to the facial surface of the teeth, coupled with calibrated archwires that exert gentle, continuous biomechanical pressure. This biological remodeling process gently moves teeth through the alveolar bone into planned alignment.

Beyond cosmetic improvements, correcting malocclusion eliminates excessive wear on enamel, reduces abnormal load on the temporomandibular joint (TMJ), and facilitates plaque control.

Clinical Indications

Fixed appliance therapy is commonly indicated for:

  • Severe dental crowding and overlapping teeth
  • Diastemas and irregular spacing between teeth
  • Overjet (excessive horizontal overlap) and deep overbite
  • Anterior or posterior crossbites causing abnormal occlusal wear
  • Rotated canines or bicuspids requiring precise torque control

The Treatment Pathway

1. Comprehensive Records & Planning

Full intraoral evaluation, facial and dental photographic series, cephalometric analysis, and dental study impressions to formulate a tailored biomechanical plan.

2. Bracket Placement & Wire Engagement

Precision bonding of brackets using adhesive resin and placement of the initial flexible nickel-titanium leveling archwire.

3. Progressive Adjustments & Consolidation

Regular visits at 4–6 week intervals to adjust wire dimensions, apply inter-arch mechanics, close extraction spaces, and detail the occlusion.

4. Tooth Debonding, Enamel Polishing & Retention

Clinical Debonding Protocol: At the completion of orthodontic alignment, fixed brackets are carefully removed using specialized debonding pliers designed to exert controlled mechanical shear force at the bracket-adhesive interface. This detaches the appliance cleanly without placing tensile stress on the underlying enamel prism structure.

Residual Resin Removal: Remaining bonding composite is gently eliminated using ultra-fine tungsten carbide finishing burs operating at controlled speeds with water coolant, followed by low-abrasion polishing paste to restore smooth enamel luster.

Stabilization & Retainers: Custom retainers (clear vacuum-formed Essix or fixed lingual retention wires) are provided to stabilize periodontal fibers and prevent physiological relapse.
