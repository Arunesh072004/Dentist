Clinical Mechanism & Safety

Professional dental bleaching employs active peroxide formulations that penetrate porous dental enamel to oxidatively break down complex carbon rings responsible for discoloration. Unlike abrasive over-the-counter charcoal or abrasive pastes, clinical whitening preserves enamel micro-structure without wearing down the protective outer tooth layer.

Prior to activating the whitening gel, our clinicians isolate and protect the marginal gingiva with a light-cured barrier resin, preventing chemical irritation and ensuring targeted bleaching of the enamel.

Common Clinical Indications

  • Extrinsic staining caused by coffee, tea, red wine, and lifestyle factors
  • Age-related enamel thinning revealing darker underlying dentin
  • Pre-restorative shade matching prior to definitive crown or veneer fabrication
  • Post-orthodontic aesthetic rejuvenation following bracket debonding

In-Office Procedure Steps

1. Pre-Treatment Polish & Baseline Shade Recording

Teeth are polished to remove extrinsic pellicle and plaque. Baseline shade is systematically recorded using a clinical dental shade guide.

2. Gingival Isolation & Barrier Application

Soft tissues, lips, and gums are shielded with cheek retractors and liquid rubber dam barrier to safeguard delicate mucous membranes.

3. Calibrated Gel Application & Monitoring

The whitening gel is applied in controlled 15-minute cycles, closely monitored by the dental surgeon to prevent tooth sensitivity.

4. Post-Treatment Fluoride Application

A desensitizing mineralizing gel is applied to nourish enamel tubules and reduce immediate post-procedure sensitivity.
