Clinical Definition & Surgical Scope

Full-mouth dental implant rehabilitation involves replacing terminal dentition or edentulous ridges across both the maxilla and mandible. When teeth are lost, alveolar bone resorption causes loss of lower facial vertical height, inward collapse of lips, and severe functional limitation.

By surgically placing strategic implant foundations and connecting them with rigid, precision-milled titanium-reinforced zirconia prostheses, we re-establish a natural bite relationship, prevent further bone loss, and restore uncompromised masticatory function.

Candidate Evaluation Criteria

  • Severe generalized periodontitis with grade III mobile, unsalvageable teeth
  • Fully edentulous patients struggling with unstable removable acrylic dentures
  • Severe functional tooth wear leading to collapsed vertical dimension and joint discomfort
  • Complex trauma or multiple failed previous root canals and crown bridges

Phased Surgical Pathway

1. Comprehensive Clinical Imaging & Diagnostic Mapping

Full-face photographs, virtual smile simulations, and cross-sectional diagnostic imaging map bone volume, nerve canals, and sinus floors for 3D surgical planning.

2. Surgical Placement & Immediate Provisional Loading

Atraumatic extraction of hopeless teeth followed by surgical insertion of 4, 6, or more implants per arch. Where primary torque stability permits, fixed provisional acrylic teeth are secured.

3. Osseointegration & Soft Tissue Maturation

A biological healing period of 3 to 6 months allows the titanium implants to rigidly fuse into surrounding bone while gingival contours mature around multi-unit abutments.

4. Precision Definitive Zirconia Delivery

Final computer-milled monolithic zirconia full-arch bridges are secured, verified for occlusion, and torqued to manufacturer specifications.
